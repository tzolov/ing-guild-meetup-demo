# EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) — condensed excerpts for the demo

[EU AI Act, Art. 5] Prohibited practices. AI systems that evaluate or classify natural persons over time based on their social behaviour or personal characteristics, leading to detrimental treatment unrelated to the context in which the data was collected (social scoring), are prohibited. So are systems that exploit the vulnerabilities of a person due to age, disability or social or economic situation.

[EU AI Act, Art. 6 and Annex III] High-risk classification. AI systems intended to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural persons or to establish their credit score are high-risk, except systems used to detect financial fraud. A provider that considers its Annex III system not to be high-risk must document that assessment before placing it on the market.

[EU AI Act, Art. 9] Risk management system. A high-risk AI system needs a continuous, iterative risk management process over its whole lifecycle: identification of known and foreseeable risks to health, safety and fundamental rights, estimation and evaluation of those risks, and adoption of risk management measures, tested before deployment.

[EU AI Act, Art. 10] Data and data governance. Training, validation and testing data of a high-risk system must be relevant, sufficiently representative and, to the best extent possible, free of errors and complete for the intended purpose. Possible biases that could lead to discrimination prohibited under Union law must be examined and mitigated. Data on protected characteristics may be processed for bias detection only under strict conditions.

[EU AI Act, Art. 13] Transparency and instructions for use. High-risk systems must be designed so that deployers can interpret the output and use it appropriately, and must come with instructions for use that state the intended purpose, the level of accuracy, known limitations and the human oversight measures.

[EU AI Act, Art. 14] Human oversight. High-risk systems must allow natural persons to oversee them effectively: to understand the system's capacities and limitations, to remain aware of automation bias, to interpret the output, to decide not to use it or to override or reverse it, and to stop it.

[EU AI Act, Art. 26] Obligations of deployers. Deployers of high-risk systems must use them according to the instructions, assign human oversight to competent persons, ensure input data is relevant and representative, monitor operation, keep the automatically generated logs for at least six months, and inform workers' representatives where applicable. Deployers taking decisions about natural persons must inform them that they are subject to the use of the high-risk system.

[EU AI Act, Art. 27] Fundamental rights impact assessment. Before deploying a high-risk Annex III system, deployers that are bodies governed by public law, or private operators providing public services, and deployers of systems that evaluate creditworthiness of natural persons, must carry out an assessment of the impact on fundamental rights: the processes in which the system is used, the period and frequency of use, the categories of persons affected, the specific risks of harm, the human oversight measures and the measures to take if risks materialise.

[EU AI Act, Art. 50] Transparency for certain AI systems. Providers must ensure that AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons are designed so that the persons are informed that they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious from the circumstances. Deployers of systems that generate or manipulate text published to inform the public must disclose that the content was artificially generated.

[EU AI Act, Art. 72] Post-market monitoring. Providers must establish a post-market monitoring system proportionate to the nature of the AI system and the risks, actively collecting and reviewing experience gained from the use of the high-risk system throughout its lifetime, in order to evaluate continued compliance.

[EU AI Act, Art. 86] Right to explanation. A person subject to a decision taken by a deployer on the basis of the output of a high-risk Annex III system, which produces legal effects or similarly significantly affects them, has the right to obtain clear and meaningful explanations of the role of the AI system in the decision and the main elements of the decision taken.
